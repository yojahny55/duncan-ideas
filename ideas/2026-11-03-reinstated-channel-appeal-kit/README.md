## 1. The Spark — This Week's Ban Saga

The creator internet spent Halloween week watching **Projekt Melody** — a six-year VTuber with 500K+ subscribers, zero strikes, zero warnings — get her YouTube channel **terminated** because a 3D modeler filed DMCA takedowns on her VODs claiming copyright over *her own avatar model*. Her appeal was **denied**. The channel only came back (Nov 1–2) after days of public escalation across X, Reddit, and press coverage.

The threads around it tell the bigger story:

- *"YouTube channels getting taken down is getting more common."* — r/VirtualYoutubers, top comment
- **Shinsogumi** (incl. DWU): entire VTuber group suspended via copyright claims — **13 members affected at once**.
- *"Ban appeal denied. Is there any hope?"* — the recurring post format across every creator community.
- LivLaw (Nov 2 2026): Uttarakhand High Court **refused** to direct YouTube to restore a channel removed after three copyright strikes — courts are not the safety net creators assume.

Melody's case is the archetype of the modern platform ban: **automated or abuse-driven enforcement, opaque reasons, a one-shot appeal process with rules that changed last month, and no tooling whatsoever for the person whose livelihood just evaporated.**

## 2. The Scale — Official Numbers, Official Blind Spots

Per Google's Transparency Report (compiled by channelterminated.com, Oct 9 2026, CC BY 4.0):

- **28,757,086 channels removed in 2024. 15,875,020 in 2025. 4,916,314 in H1 2026 alone** (Q2 2026: 2.73M).
- 84.5% of Q2 2026 removals were "spam" — but the official count **excludes copyright terminations entirely** (the Melody category is literally invisible in the data).
- Only **6.9% of appealed videos** were reinstated in Q2 2026.
- **There is no official success rate for channel termination appeals.** YouTube says only "most termination decisions are upheld."

Even if 90% of removals are spam farms, the remainder is **hundreds of thousands of real channels per year** — plus every hacked-then-terminated channel (a documented epidemic: hijacker uploads → strikes → termination → the victim is punished for the hack).

## 3. The Rules Just Changed and Nobody Told the Creators

The appeal process is a moving target that punishes people for following last year's advice:

| Rule | Reality (Oct–Nov 2026) | What creators believe |
|---|---|---|
| Appeal count | **Two appeals within 1 year** (Help Center, since ~end of Sept 2026) | "One appeal, then it's over" (TeamYouTube FAQ from Nov 2025 still online) |
| Repeat text | Second appeal with the same text → **automated reply**, wastes the attempt | "Wait two weeks and resubmit" (forum advice from a retired form) |
| Spam terminations | **30-day grace period test** announced Oct 30 2026: fix issues or lose the channel | Doesn't exist in most guides yet |
| New channels | Creating one = **circumvention** → makes the ban worse and kills Second Chances | "Just start over" (the single most destructive advice) |
| Second Chances | New channel possible **12 months** after termination — **not for copyright terminations** | Widely unknown |
| EU creators | Free **DSA internal complaint** (≥6 months window) + certified dispute bodies | Almost nobody uses it |
| India creators | Grievance Officer must resolve in **7 days**; **GAC appeal within 30 days** — and the platform *must follow* GAC decisions | Unknown; courts refusing instead |

A terminated creator in fight-or-flight mode cannot track two appeals, a one-year window, a 30-day grace test, a 12-month Second Chances date, and jurisdiction-specific legal clocks that run on *days*. They miss deadlines they didn't know existed, and they burn their one good appeal on generic text.

## 4. What Exists Today (and Why It Fails)

- **channelterminated.com** — excellent editorial + free deadline calculator. Proves demand, publishes *no product, no per-case tooling, and explicitly refuses to publish success rates* ("we do not invent one"). The crowd-data gap is theirs by choice — and ours by opportunity.
- **SEO blog guides** (instantmonetize, youreputationsolution, messagemyfans) — static, already stale on the two-appeal change, monetized by "reputation management" services of dubious quality.
- **TeamYouTube on X** — can explain and flag; **cannot overturn policy decisions**. Melody's reinstatement needed viral pressure, not the official channel.
- **Lawyers** — only viable for channels with big revenue; "a lawyer's letter occasionally triggers a review" is a Hail Mary, not a process.
- **YouTube's own appeal form** — no visibility, no feedback loop, no drafting help, no deadline tracking.

**Nobody owns the creator's "I just got terminated" moment end-to-end.** InReview owns it for app-store submissions; Reinstated owns it for platform enforcement.

## 5. The Product — TurboTax for Platform Bans

**Reinstated** = case-file workflow + deadline engine + AI appeal drafter + crowd outcome intelligence.

### 5.1 Ban Decoder (the free wedge, 90 seconds)
Paste the termination email or drop a screenshot → Claude classifies:
- **Reason taxonomy:** 3-strike copyright / single severe violation / spam-policy / hacked-then-terminated / DMCA abuse (the Melody class) / monetization-YPP (not a termination — different ladder)
- **Exact policy cited** vs what the email *implies* (creators routinely misread which one matters)
- **Which ladder you're on:** termination appeal vs strike appeal vs account recovery vs DSA/GAC — "check what exactly was rejected" is the #1 confusion per the appeal-rejected guides
- Outputs your **Case Card**: reason, platform posture, remaining appeals, next deadline. Shareable, anonymous.

### 5.2 Deadline Engine (every clock, one strip)
Computes and watches: 2-appeal counter, 1-year window, spam 30-day grace expiry, Second Chances month-12 date (with copyright-termination ineligibility warning), EU DSA 6-month complaint window, India GAC 30-day clock, withheld-revenue notes. Escalation ladder reminders: D-30 / D-7 / D-1 / expired.

### 5.3 Appeal Drafter (the moment that decides everything)
- Drafts the appeal to answer **the exact policy named in the email**, with facts a reviewer can verify (project files, licenses, upload metadata, account-recovery proof).
- **New-Facts Mode** for appeal #2: the tool refuses to generate a reworded duplicate ("same text = auto-reply = wasted appeal") and instead runs an evidence checklist to find genuinely new material — completed hack recovery, license documents, the wrong-policy misread.
- **DMCA Abuse Kit** (the Melody flow): counter-notification language, license-chain reconstruction, modeler/vendor contract templates, false-claim perjury-notice reminder.
- **Hacked-Channel Triage:** recovery-first sequence (Google account recovery → then appeal, with the recovery ticket cited as new facts).

### 5.4 Evidence Vault (sell the insurance BEFORE the ban)
The Melody lesson: she couldn't prove her rights to her own VODs fast enough. Vault auto-ingests (YouTube Data API + Google Takeout): video metadata, licenses, brand deals, asset provenance (3D model licenses, music licenses), revenue history, prior-good-standing record. Weekly snapshot = provable "channel history" timestamped before any dispute. This is the **pre-ban subscription** — the insurance that makes the post-ban case winnable.

### 5.5 Crowd Outcome Intelligence (the house moat)
Every case contributes anonymized outcome data: reinstatement rate **by reason × appeal number × jurisdiction × channel size × evidence type attached**. The dataset channelterminated.com refuses to build and YouTube will never publish:
- *"DMCA-abuse terminations with a signed model license attached: 61% reinstated on appeal #1 (n=412)."*
- *"Spam-flagged channels in the 30-day grace test: 78% survived after full library cleanup."*
- Public **Ban Wave Tracker**: terminations per week by reason, self-reported + search-interest correlated — inherently viral dev/creator-Twitter content every time a wave hits.

### 5.6 The Never-Do List (harm reduction, free forever)
New channel = circumvention. Deleting the Google account closes Second Chances. Mass re-filing triggers auto-replies. Public shaming without a filed appeal wastes the window (Melody got lucky; the crowd data will show who doesn't).

## 6. Why This Isn't an Existing Idea (Duplicate Check)

- **NOT UnlockMe** (2026-04-03): credential lockouts — can't *log in*. Reinstated is post-termination *policy enforcement* — you can log in, your channel is gone. Different moment, different machinery (appeals/law vs recovery forms/2FA).
- **NOT InReview** (2026-10-30): app-store submission *queue* telemetry for devs. Reinstated is enforcement *appeals* for creators. Same house pattern (crowd percentiles + playbook + deadline clocks), different platform, audience, and moment — InReview is "waiting," Reinstated is "convicted."
- **NOT SaidSo** (2026-10-07): evidence capture for customer-service promises. Complementary posture, different dispute.
- **NOT CeaseFire / HOA Shield / FlightDrop**: same consumer-rights DNA (scripts + deadlines + escalation), entirely different adversary.
- **NOT BotBustr / ProofPost / any creator-analytics idea**: those grow channels; Reinstated saves them.

## 9. Design (ui-ux-pro-max)

**"Case file meets mission control."** A terminated creator is grieving and panicking — the UI must feel like a competent paralegal took the file, not like another dashboard.

- **Palette:** ink navy `#0B0E14` (night-of-the-ban dark mode native), dossier cream `#F0E9D8`, stamp red `#E5484D` (DENIED / TERMINATED), reinstated green `#3FB950`, deadline amber `#FFB454`, redaction black `#16181D`.
- **Type:** Fraunces for case headings (legal-document warmth), Inter for prose, JetBrains Mono for all dates, counters, and cited policy codes.
- **Signature components:**
  - **The Case File** — manila folder tab with your channel name; every screen is a document inside it.
  - **Stamp animations** — TERMINATED (red, the truth on day one) → APPEAL #1 FILED → REINSTATED (green, the only confetti this app will ever fire).
  - **Deadline strip** — horizontal clock rail across the top of every screen: two appeal seals, the 1-year window bar, jurisdiction clocks.
  - **Appeal typewriter** — the draft renders as a formal letter with policy citations highlighted in amber; copy-to-clipboard + PDF export.
  - **Odds table** — crowd stats rendered as evidence tags ("with license attached: 61%").
- **Voice:** the calm paralegal. Never "don't worry," always "here is your next step, here is the deadline, here is what not to do."
- **Every screen ends in ONE action:** Decode, File, Wait, or Escalate.
