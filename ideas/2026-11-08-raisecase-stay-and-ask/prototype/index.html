The first 90 seconds

Send the brief 48 hours ahead. Ask 6–8 weeks before the cycle. Then open with evidence, not apology.

Opening — verbatim

"I sent you a one-pager — thanks for reading it ahead. I'm not looking elsewhere and I'm not issuing an ultimatum. I've led the migration, rebuilt onboarding, and trained five hires since my last review. The market band for this scope is $90 to $130k; I'm asking for $88k — deliberately below the band. If base is frozen this cycle, page two has three alternatives I'd also say yes to."

Objection plays · crowd-tested
"The budget is tight this year." HEARD 61%▾
Never accept a soft no. Convert it into a dated commitment.
"Understood. When does the next cycle open — and can we write down today what I need to have delivered by then to make $88k a yes?"
→ 71% of users who got written criteria received a raise at the next cycle (n=318)
"You're too valuable where you are." HEARD 22%▾
The compliment trap. Thank them, then price the value.
"I appreciate that — and I've made myself replaceable on the day-to-day: the playbook runs without me now. That's exactly why the scope I'm carrying is the senior band, and why I'm asking to be paid for it."
→ pairs with the handover template in the Brief (page 2)
"If we raise yours, everyone will want one." HEARD 14%▾
They're inviting you to argue fairness. Don't. Argue scope.
"I can only speak to my scope, and the benchmark is attached: this band is for the role as it exists today — migration, onboarding, training. I'm not asking to be treated specially; I'm asking to be priced accurately."
→ benchmark-cited asks succeed at 2× the rate of fairness-cited asks (n=842)
