What's wrong, in plain English

1. The ER visit is billed twice. Line 2 is an exact duplicate of line 1 — same code, same date, same $860.
Duplicate billing is the single most common hospital error. Insurance paid once; the provider is billing you twice.
Ref: EOB adj. code CO-16 · patient owes $0 on duplicate
2. X-ray upcoded: 71047 → 71045. Your bill lists "2 views" but the EOB shows the plan processed a single-view code — and the radiology report in your portal says one view was taken.
Upcoding inflates the allowed amount and, in high-deductible plans, what lands on you. The radiology report is your receipt.
Ref: request corrected claim w/ CPT 71045
3. Balance billing on the nurse observation line. The $310 out-of-network line at an in-network ER facility is exactly what the No Surprises Act prohibits — your cost-share must equal the in-network rate.
This is the strongest card you hold. Providers know it; they bank on you not knowing it.
Ref: No Surprises Act §149(a) · 42 U.S.C. 300gg-111
